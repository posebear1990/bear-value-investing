# Economic Moat, Risk Transmission & Cognitive Bias Checklist

This document provides a reference checklist for evaluating competitive moats, supply-chain/counterparty risk transmission, cognitive biases, and downside risks.

---

## 1. Economic Moat Evaluation (5 Pillar Moat Framework)
1. **Network Effects**: Does the value of the product increase exponentially as more users join? (e.g., platforms, payment networks).
2. **Switching Costs**: Is it expensive, painful, or risky for customers to switch to a competitor? (e.g., enterprise software, core banking).
3. **Cost Advantage**: Can the company produce goods/services at a lower cost than competitors due to scale, location, or unique assets?
4. **Intangible Assets**: Does the company possess strong pricing power driven by brand loyalty, patents, or regulatory licenses?
5. **Efficient Scale**: Is the market size limited such that only 1 or 2 dominant players can operate profitably?

---

## 2. Supply-Chain & Counterparty Risk Transmission (产业链与交易对手传导检查) ⚡️

Do NOT restrict risk checks solely to the target company's official announcements. Perform a 360-degree supply-chain and counterparty spillover evaluation:

1. **Upstream & Downstream Counterparty Risk**:
   - Are key suppliers, major customers, joint venture partners, or M&A targets facing sanctions, investigations, bank liquidity squeezes, or shipping restrictions?
   - Is a major customer diversifying orders to competitors, causing structural revenue erosion?
2. **Cross-Border Regulatory & Sanctions Conflicts**:
   - US/EU Sanctions, Entity Lists, OFAC/SDN listings, export controls, or customs blockades affecting the company's ecosystem.
   - Domestic anti-monopoly enforcement, data privacy, or platform governance rules.
   - Is the company caught in a "sandwich trap" between conflicting jurisdictional regulations?
3. **Industry Price Wars & Margin Erosion**:
   - Are major competitors waging aggressive price, subsidy, or capacity wars that destroy the industry profit pool (e.g., food delivery price wars)?

---

## 3. Cognitive Bias Audit & Self-Correction (认知偏差与心理自我纠偏) ⚡️

During initial research (`INIT_BUY`) or portfolio audit (`AUDIT_PORTFOLIO`), actively screen for common investor cognitive biases:

1. **"Monopoly Mirage" Bias (天然垄断幻觉)**:
   - *Warning*: mistaking high market share or natural monopoly for an untouchable moat, while ignoring that extreme dominance attracts regulatory antitrust investigations (e.g., Trip.com antitrust case).
2. **"Falling Price = Bargain" Bias (跌多即便宜/把利空误当长期无关)**:
   - *Warning*: assuming every price drop is a buying opportunity, without checking if the underlying moat or profit pool has been permanently damaged.
3. **"Paper Profit vs Cash Flow" Bias (纸面盈利与真实现金流偏差)**:
   - *Warning*: relying on reported Net Income while ignoring negative Free Cash Flow or bloated Accounts Receivable / CapEx.
4. **"Speculation 退化为 长期持有" Bias (投机套牢退化为被动持仓)**:
   - *Warning*: holding a short-term theme/event play (e.g., AI glasses speculation) after the catalyst has passed or failed, instead of exiting as originally planned.

---

## 4. Cyclicality & Value Trap Verification
- **Peak Earnings Warning**: Is current low P/E driven by temporary peak commodity/industry cycle prices?
- **Technological Disruption Risk**: Is the business model facing structural decline?
- **Debt Refinancing Risk**: Does the company have heavy debt maturing within 12-24 months in a high-interest environment?

---

## 5. Management & Capital Allocation Check
- **Capital Allocation Track Record**: Does management buy back shares when stock is undervalued, or engage in value-destroying M&A?
- **Insider Ownership & Governance**: Do executives hold significant skin-in-the-game? Are compensation incentives aligned with Free Cash Flow per share?
